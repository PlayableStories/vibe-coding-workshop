# The Meaning Worksheet

Five minutes here will save you an hour of building. Before you touch a single tool, get clear on *what your game is about.* You can scribble this on paper, in your phone notes, anywhere.

The whole goal is to be able to finish **one sentence**. Everything else is in service of it.

---

## 1. The feeling

What's one thing you want a player to *feel* by the end? Not learn — feel.

> _(e.g. "the quiet ache of leaving somewhere", "how absurd my job is", "the relief of slowing down")_

**My feeling:** ______________________________________________

## 2. The thing it's about

Where does that feeling come from in your life? A memory, a frustration, a worry, something in the news?

**My subject:** ______________________________________________

## 3. The one sentence

Now join them. Fill this in:

> **"My game is about ____________, and I want the player to feel ____________."**

If you can say that out loud and it feels true, you're ready. If it's fuzzy, keep poking at 1 and 2 — the fuzz always shows up later as a game that doesn't quite land.

---

## 4. Which game gets you there fastest?

Look at the **[catalogue](02-pick-a-game.md)** and pick the game whose *feeling* is closest to yours. You'll change everything on top — this is just your launchpad.

**Starting from:** ______________________________________________

## 5. Reskin or remix?

- **Reskin** (Rung 1): the existing rules already fit my feeling — I just need to change the words and look.
- **Remix** (Rung 2): the meaning I want needs a *different rule.*

**My plan:** ______________________________________________

## 6. (If remixing) The one rule

What single rule will you bend, and what will it make the game mean?

> **"Instead of ____________, now ____________, which makes the game about ____________."**

---

## The test you'll come back to

When your game is built, play it as a stranger and ask the only question that matters:

> **Did they feel the thing in my one sentence?**

Not "is it polished." Not "is it clever." Did the feeling land. That's the whole craft.
