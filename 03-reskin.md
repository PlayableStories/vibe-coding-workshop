# Reskin It (Rung 1)

This is where you go from *someone else's game* to *your game*. You'll keep the rules exactly as they are, and change the words and the look so it tells your story. By the end of this page you'll have a real, playable thing with your name on it.

Take a breath. You can't break anything that can't be undone.

## Step 1 — Open the game in Replit

1. Go to **[replit.com](https://replit.com)** and sign in.
2. Click **Create** (or **+ Create Repl**).
3. Choose **Import from GitHub**.
4. Paste the **Import URL** of the game you picked from the **[catalogue](02-pick-a-game.md)**, for example:
   ```
   https://github.com/PlayableStories/memory-of-home
   ```
5. Replit will pull the game in. Give it a moment.

## Step 2 — Run it once, as-is

Press the big **Run** button at the top. A preview of the game appears. Play it for a minute.

This matters: you want to feel the game *before* you change it, so you know what you're changing. If Run does anything confusing, that's normal — see **[fixing things](prompts/fixing-things.md)**.

## Step 3 — Meet the AI

Find the **AI / Assistant / Agent** panel in Replit (usually a chat box on the side). This is who you'll be talking to. You don't touch the code — you tell the AI what you want, in plain English, and it does the editing.

Try this first, just to get comfortable:

> *Give me a plain-English summary of what this game is and how it plays. I'm not a coder — keep it simple.*

Read its answer. Now you both know what you're working with.

## Step 4 — Reskin, one layer at a time

Don't ask for everything at once. Change one thing, look at it, then change the next. Here's a good order. (All of these, and more, are in the **[reskin prompt library](prompts/reskin-prompts.md)** — copy and adapt.)

**a) The words / story.** This is the biggest change.
> *I want to re-theme this game. Instead of [original theme], it's now about [your theme]. Rewrite all the text the player sees — titles, instructions, story fragments, endings — to fit. Keep the rules exactly the same.*

**b) The feeling of the look.** Don't worry about exact colours — describe the *mood*.
> *Change the colour palette so the game feels [warm and nostalgic / cold and corporate / tense / dreamlike]. Keep it readable.*

**c) The details.** Names, labels, the title at the top, the wording of the ending.
> *Rename [the four meters / the ranks / the objects] to [your list].*

After each change, press **Run** and look. If you like it, keep going. If you don't:
> *Undo that last change — I preferred it before.*

## Step 5 — Make it feel finished

A few small touches separate "a demo" from "a thing I made":

> *Update the title and the opening screen to set the mood I want.*

> *Write a closing line that leaves the player with [the one feeling from my worksheet].*

> *Check that nothing still mentions the original [CEO / pills / home] theme by accident.*

## You're at Rung 1. That's a real game.
