# Publish & Share

You made a thing. Now let's get it a link you can send to anyone — a friend, your family, the whole room.

## Publish from Replit

Replit can put your game on the open web for free. The exact button moves around, but the flow is always:

1. Make sure the game **Runs** and plays the way you want.
2. Look for **Deploy** / **Publish** (often near the Run button or in a menu).
3. Choose the **free / static / Autoscale** option if asked — for these small games, the cheapest option is plenty. Ask your facilitator if you're unsure which to pick.
4. Replit gives you a **link** (something like `your-game.replit.app`). That's your game, live, on the internet.

If you get stuck, just ask the AI:

> *Help me publish this game to a public link I can share. I'm not a coder — walk me through each click.*

## Or publish to itch.io

[itch.io](https://itch.io) is where a lot of small, personal games live. It gives your game its own page, with a title, a description and a cover image, and people can play it right there in the browser. It's free, and a good home for your game after tonight. It's also the best link for **[Storymode Shorts](https://shorts.intostorymode.com)**, which can show itch.io games on its own pages.

You'll need a free itch.io account. Making one before you arrive saves a few minutes.

### 1. Ask the AI for a ZIP

However you built your game, ask the AI that built it:

> *I want to upload this game to itch.io so people can play it in their browser. Package it as a ZIP file that's ready to upload. Do whatever building it needs, and make sure it will work when itch.io runs it. Then tell me how to download the ZIP. I'm not a coder, so keep it simple.*

When you have the ZIP, open it. You should see `index.html` straight away, not a folder. If it's inside a folder, ask the AI to fix it.

### 2. Make the itch.io page

1. On itch.io, open the menu by your name and choose **Upload new project**.
2. Give it a **title**. This also sets your link, like `yourname.itch.io/your-game`.
3. Under **Kind of project**, choose **HTML**.
4. Under **Uploads**, upload your ZIP, then tick **This file will be played in the browser**.
5. Under **Embed options**, set the size to fit your game, and tick **Mobile friendly** and **Fullscreen button** if you see them.
6. Add your **one sentence** to the description. That's the most important line on the page.
7. Under **Visibility**, choose **Public**. New pages start as **Draft**, which only you can see, so a draft link won't work for anyone else.
8. Press **Save**, then **View page**, and play it once.

**A blank or black screen?** Ask the AI that built it: *"My game shows a blank screen on itch.io. Here's what happens: [describe it]. itch.io runs the game from its own web address, so check that every file path is relative and that `index.html` is at the top of the ZIP, then give me a new ZIP."* Upload the new ZIP. Uploading a new version replaces the old one and keeps the same link.

## Test your link

Open the link on your **phone**, or send it to the person next to you. Play it as a stranger would. Does it make sense without you standing there explaining it? Does the *feeling* land?

That last question is the only review that matters. Not "is it polished" — *"did they feel the thing I meant?"*

## Submit it to Shorts

Share what you made with everyone who comes after you:

1. Open **[shorts.intostorymode.com/submit](https://shorts.intostorymode.com/submit)**.
2. Add your game's title, your live link (your itch.io link if you have one), and the **one sentence** of what it's about.
3. Answer the credit and consent questions however feels right to you — it's entirely your call.

Do this while you're still in the room — it takes two minutes here, and much longer to chase
by email later.

## Tell the story behind it (60 seconds)

If we do a show-and-tell, you don't need to demo every feature. Just answer three things:

1. **What did you start from?** (a catalogue game, or your own idea)
2. **What did you change?** (the reskin, the one rule you bent, or what you built from scratch)
3. **What's it about?** (your one sentence)

That's a complete creative story. People will remember the *one sentence* far longer than the gameplay.
