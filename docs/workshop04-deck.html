The big idea

The mechanic is the message.

A game's real story isn't the words on screen — it's the rules. Change the rule, change the meaning.

A rule creates a feeling

What did you do? What did it make you feel? Which rule caused it?

  • Helping someone costs you points → sacrifice
  • Never quite catching up → the treadmill of modern work
The framework

Intention → Player Experience → Action → System Response → Consequence

Don't ask what game should I make?

Ask what should a player feel? — then find the action, and the response, that creates it.

The framework, on Boardroom
IntentionShow that leadership is an impossible balancing act
Player experienceConstant, uncomfortable trade-offs
ActionSwipe left or right on a decision
System responseFour meters move, some up and some down
ConsequencePush any meter too far and it all collapses, so every later choice gets harder
Three things to keep in mind
  • You design, AI assists. The ideas and decisions are yours.
  • A working game isn't automatically a meaningful one. Does it say what you meant?
  • Playtesting is part of making. Watch someone else play.

The Mini Game Spec · every mode uses it
  1. Intention — what should the player feel, notice, question or understand?
  2. Player action — what does the player do?
  3. System response — what happens because of that action?
  4. Consequence — how does that change what comes next?
  5. Player flow — how does it begin, develop and end?
  6. Scope — what's the smallest version worth testing?

Paper prototype, then swap
  1. Make it playable on the table: cards, dice, tokens, sticky notes, a sketch.
  2. One person plays. Another is "the computer" and applies the rules.
  3. Swap with another pair. Play theirs without being told what it's about.
  4. Tell them what you experienced. They change one rule.

Keep it small: one action, one response, one consequence.

How we co-create

The group decides.

  • Intention keeper — "does this still say what we meant?"
  • Playtester — plays each version fresh, says what they notice
  • Prompter — types the group's decisions. Take turns.

Play every version before asking for changes. Change one thing at a time.
