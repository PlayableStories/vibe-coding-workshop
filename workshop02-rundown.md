Welcome! Over the next two and a half hours you'll make a small browser game that *means something* — without writing a single line of code. You'll talk to an AI in plain English, and it builds the game. Your job is the part no machine can do: deciding what the game is *about*, and what you want a player to feel.

**The one big idea:**

> **The mechanic is the message.** A game's real story isn't the words on screen — it's the *rules*. Change the rule, change the meaning.

**What you'll leave with:** a published, playable link to a game that's yours — and a workflow you can keep using.

### 6:00 · Welcome & the Big Idea (10 mins)

* Hello from William Wong & Into Storymode.
* Why games are a powerful medium: you don't *read* the message, you *do* it — and you feel it.
* We'll play a game or two together and find the *one rule* that carries the meaning.

> **The mechanic is the message.** Two examples you'll play:
> * **Boardroom** ([play ▶](https://corporate-reign.vercel.app)) — four meters (Shareholders, Management, Staff, Public Image) all start at 50, and every decision pleases one group while angering another. The rule itself says: *leadership is an impossible balancing act.*
> * **Office Chair Racing** ([play ▶](https://office-race-game-lilac.vercel.app)) — you pump the keys to go faster, and the moment you stop, you're fired; every promotion just makes your rivals more aggressive. The rule says: *climbing the ladder doesn't make you safe.*

### 6:20 · What Makes a Game *Mean* Something (10 mins)

Short games that still hit hard. How a rule creates a feeling:

* Move only *forward*, never back → **regret**
* Helping someone *costs* you points → **sacrifice**
* Never quite catching up → the **treadmill of modern work**

Why a tiny game can land an idea differently from an article, video, or slide — you *enact* it.

### 6:40 · Break the Convention (Pair Exercise) (15 mins)

Same insight as last time, but you find it yourselves this time.

**5 min, pairs — list the conventions.** What do games train you to expect, without ever saying so? *A health bar goes down when you're hurt. A locked door needs a key. Your score goes up. The timer counts down. You have three lives.*

**5 min, pairs — break one.** Pick a convention from your list and ask: what if it didn't work that way? What would that make a player feel?

**5 min — room debrief.** Share the best ones. This is exactly what every game in today's catalogue already does — Pill Match breaks "matching earns points," Memory of Home breaks "a matched memory is whole."

**The framework underneath it — work backwards from the feeling:**

> **Message → Experience → Mechanic**

Don't ask *"What game should I make?"* Ask *"What should my audience feel or experience?"* — then find the rule that creates it, or the expectation that breaks it.

**The ladder you'll climb today:**
1. **Reskin** — keep the rules, change the words & look. *Most people start here, and it's plenty.*
2. **Remix** — find the expectation the game relies on, and break it. The magic rung.
3. **Rebuild** — describe a whole new game from scratch. If you're flying.

## Activity 1 · 6:55 — Story First (10 mins)

Five minutes here saves an hour of building. Open the **[worksheet](worksheet.md)** (paper or phone) and finish **one sentence**:

> **"My game is about ____________, and I want the player to feel ____________."**

Draw from a personal experience, a community story, a social issue, or a mission. Then say it out loud to a neighbour — saying it reveals the fuzz.

*Stuck? Ask yourself: what annoyed you this week? Funny or angry is a great seed.*

---

## Activity 2 · 7:05 — Mechanic Design (15 mins)

Turn your sentence into a playable interaction.

* What should players *do*?
* What choices will they make?
* What emotion should the rule create?

| Your story | A mechanic that carries it |
|---|---|
| Feeling unheard | Dialogue choices that get ignored |
| Housing insecurity | Constantly moving between spaces |
| Memory & migration | Matching fragments of memories |
| Community cooperation | Shared resource management |

**Pick your launchpad** from the **[catalogue](02-pick-a-game.md)** — the game whose *feeling* is closest to yours:

> Angry or funny about work → **Boardroom** · Pressure and burnout → **Office Chair Racing** · Doing the right *amount* of something → **Pill Match** · Tender and personal → **Memory of Home** · Reflective, about what you'd live with → **Building On** · About a force you can't control → **Ballot / Waste**

Turn to your neighbour and tell them which one you're leaning toward, and why — hearing your own reason out loud is often the moment it clicks.

Finish the worksheet: **Reskin or Remix?**

## Activity 3 · 7:20 — Build with AI (45 mins)

This is the hands-on heart of the session. We'll go together at the start, then you'll build at your own pace. Facilitators float throughout — wave us over any time.

**1 · Import & Run (together).** In [Replit](https://replit.com): **Create → Import from GitHub**, paste your game's Import URL from the [catalogue](02-pick-a-game.md), press **Run**, and play it once as-is. Meet the AI panel with: *"Give me a plain-English summary of this game. I'm not a coder — keep it simple."*

**2 · Reskin (Rung 1).** One change at a time — ask, Run, look, repeat. *"Re-theme this game. Instead of [original], it's now about [your theme]. Rewrite all the text the player sees. Keep the rules exactly the same."* → More in **[reskin prompts](prompts/reskin-prompts.md)**.

**3 · Remix (Rung 2) — the dream, not the requirement.** Find the expectation your game relies on, and break it. **Half of Me** is the proof: it takes Memory of Home — where two cards match if they share the same object *and* the same colour — and breaks a single expectation, that a matched memory is whole. Now two cards match on object *or* colour, never both, so every memory you recover is incomplete. One broken expectation, a whole new game.

Now try your own: *"I want to change one rule. Right now [current rule]. I want it to instead [new rule], because I want the game to feel like [your meaning]. Keep everything else working."* Then Run and *play it* — does the feeling land? → More in **[remix prompts](prompts/remix-prompts.md)**.

> 💡 Your first remix might miss. **That's the workshop working, not failing** — find a different expectation and try again.
>
> 🛠️ Stuck, or the AI changed too much? Undo with *"Undo that last change — I preferred it before,"* and see **[fixing things](prompts/fixing-things.md)**.

### 8:05 · Publish, Share & Reflect (15 mins)

**Publish:** In Replit, hit **Deploy / Publish** (free option) to get a link like `your-game.replit.app`. Open it on your phone. → **[Publish & share](05-publish-and-share.md)**

**Submit it to Shorts:** While you're still in the room, open **[shorts.intostorymode.com/submit](https://shorts.intostorymode.com/submit)** and add your game — title, link, and your one sentence. Answer the credit and consent questions however feels right to you.

**Show-and-tell — 60 seconds each:**
1. What did you start from?
2. What did you change? (the reskin, or the expectation you broke)
3. What's it about? (your one sentence)

While everyone's still here and pleased with what they made, three plain questions for anyone who built something today — the same ones on the Shorts form:

- May we use your name?
- May we link to your game?
- May we quote what you told us?

However you answer is entirely fine — say so now, in the room, rather than us chasing you by email later.

**Reflect:** What surprised you? How could this apply in your organisation or community?
